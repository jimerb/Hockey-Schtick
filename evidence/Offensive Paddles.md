# Offensive paddle mechanics prototype — 0.4.5

Two short paddles are hinged into the straight far-zone boards, at matching positions just beyond the far blue line. Each is 1.85 units long and 0.16 wide, compared with the lower flippers' 4.45 length and 0.95 width. They rest along the boards, entirely behind the existing collision face. The board artwork stays intact while their mechanics are tested. Red/cream resting faces, a dark mounting recess and a metal hinge sit within the existing board area; a flush top rim makes them readable from the elevated camera. The resting faces hide during deployment to reveal the moving paddles.

The two side controls now operate the same-side upper and lower paddles together. By default A controls the left pair and L controls the right pair; custom bindings and on-screen side buttons operate the same pairs. Space has no match action, and the former shared Upper Paddles button is removed. Each upper paddle has independent angle, hold and return state, including independent interpolation and resting-face visibility. Holding a side button keeps its upper paddle fully extended after the inward stroke; releasing returns it to the boards while the other side can stay held. A quick tap receives a 12-tick (100 ms) upper stroke so it completes the swing. There is no one-shot timeout or retrigger cooldown: releasing and repressing during return immediately starts another swing. Keyboard repeat cannot retrigger a hold, and releasing one input source preserves another simultaneous keyboard/pointer hold. Cancellation clears that source from both same-side paddles. Escape pauses and resumes; Play or Enter starts a match. Goals, resets, focus loss and pause clear both pairs and recess the upper paddles. Lower-flipper dynamics and the original 10-tick tap timing are retained. The automatic browser demo also drives its same-side upper paddles.

The puck responds through actual moving-collider contact. No attraction, auto-aim, remote impulse, extra puck, or scoring assist is used. Strike timing/contact position determine whether the puck shoots toward goal, banks, or misses. Collision shapes extend below the ice to avoid an underside trap, and the existing puck-speed limit and match recovery safeguards still apply. The practice rink does not gain upper paddles.
